## Why these thresholds are binding

Phase 3 exists partly to prevent Phase 4 from becoming an exercise in changing
rules until an attractive equation survives. The thresholds below were
calibrated on 40 null worlds **before** any real-data search
exists. If Phase 4 discovers that a frozen rule is genuinely invalid, that must
be handled as a documented deviation with **conservative** interpretation — not
by substituting a rule that happens to admit the candidate in hand.

Binary `+`, `-`, `*`, `/`; unary
`sqrt`, `log`, `square`, `cube`, `inv`; `exp`,
trigonometric functions and free-exponent `^` excluded; maximum complexity
**20**. Phase 4 may **not widen the grammar** without
recalibrating the null thresholds — a larger hypothesis space invalidates the
table below.

## Acceptance rule

1. validation R^2 exceeds the null threshold at the candidate's own complexity
2. selection frequency >= 20/30 seeds
3. complexity <= 20
4. invalid fraction <= 0.005
5. the complete required falsification set passes

A candidate additionally **claims structure beyond mass** only if:

1. variable support contains a non-mass descriptor
2. ablating the non-mass descriptors destroys the result
3. the mass block alone does not reproduce the result

## Binding Phase 4 restrictions

- positive mode only — Phase 2's structure-beyond-mass result does not replicate in negative mode (K4B FAIL, +1.93%, CI spans zero)
- no mechanistic reading may lean on RT-correlated descriptors (NC7)
- no full-corpus preprocessing-invariance claim is available (39 compounds, 7.1%)
- a mass-associated candidate must be reported as a mass law unless the structural-claim rule is satisfied
- thresholds frozen here are binding; a genuinely invalid rule must be handled as a documented deviation with conservative interpretation, not silently replaced
