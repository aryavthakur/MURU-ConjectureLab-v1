### Ringless / empty-scaffold policy

Bemis-Murcko is **undefined** for a molecule with no ring system:
`GetScaffoldForMol` returns an empty molecule. Keying on the returned
SMILES would place every acyclic compound into one group named by the
empty string, asserting a shared core that does not exist.

**Policy.** Each of the 32 ringless compounds becomes its own
scaffold group, keyed by its connectivity block. They are **not** pooled.

**Declared consequence.** S2 is marginally *easier* for those
5.8% of compounds, because scaffold-disjointness is
undefined for them. S3 clusters them properly by similarity and is the
stress test that covers them. This was recorded before any performance
was observed (`MASTER_PLAN_CLARIFICATIONS.md` C5).

### S2 verdict

**Pathological: False.** 368 scaffold groups over 549 compounds; largest group holds 80 (14.6%). Neither degenerate nor dominated, so S2 is meaningful for this corpus and is pre-registered as the primary split.

## Similarity clustering (S3)

RDKit's `Butina.ClusterData` takes a **distance** cutoff, not a
similarity. The pre-registered Tanimoto **similarity** threshold is
**0.6**, applied as distance cutoff
**0.4** (`distance = 1 - similarity`).

Passing 0.6 directly as a distance would have clustered everything above
Tanimoto similarity 0.4 — a far looser grouping that would have made the
cluster-disjoint split silently easier. `tests/test_splits.py` pins the
conversion with synthetic bit vectors of hand-computed similarity,
including a regression test that a pair at similarity 0.4 is **not**
merged under a 0.6 similarity threshold.

## Leakage canary (S0)

S0 assigns folds per **row**, so a molecule's six energies land on both
sides of the wall — the trivial failure mode the project files warn about.

Fraction of compounds split across folds under S0: **100.0%**.
The canary therefore does exercise the intended failure mechanism.
