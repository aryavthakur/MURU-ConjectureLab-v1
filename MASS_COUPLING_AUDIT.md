Precursor mass appears in the definition of the response:

```
mu = (sum_k I_k * mz_k) / (sum_k I_k) / mz_precursor
```

so an apparent association between mu and precursor mass may be produced
**mechanically by the normalization** rather than by chemistry. This audit
asks one question:

> Can the definition of mu itself produce an apparent association with
> precursor mass even when the underlying **fractional** fragmentation
> behaviour is held constant?

It is a Phase 2 response-variable audit. It is **not** the Phase 3
synthetic discovery system: there is no search, no hidden ground-truth
equation, and no discovery engine.

## Analytic statement

Writing SY for survival yield and phi for the intensity-weighted mean
fragment mass as a fraction of the precursor mass,

```
mu = SY + (1 - SY) * phi,   phi = <mz_fragment> / mz_precursor
```

(exactly, up to the ~9 ppm observed/declared precursor ratio Phase 1
quantified as deviation D1). Both terms are dimensionless. Therefore:

- **Fractional regime.** If every fragment's m/z is a fixed proportion of
  its precursor and relative intensities are fixed, phi is mass-invariant
  and mu carries **no** mass dependence.
- **Absolute regime.** If fragment m/z is set on an absolute scale,
  `phi ~ 1/mz_precursor`, so mu acquires a negative mass association
  **mechanically, with no chemistry at all**.

## Deterministic diagnostic

Real precursor masses from the development corpus (549 compounds,
116.0–837.5 m/z) are combined with a fragmentation
programme that is **identical for every compound** — the same survival
yield per energy, the same relative fragment intensities. Only the *scale*
on which fragment masses are placed changes between regimes.

| Regime | rho(mu, precursor m/z) at NCE 90 | max abs rho | Construction |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A_fractional** | -0.0578 | 0.0688 | Purely fractional fragmentation: fragment m/z proportional to precursor m/z. Fractional behaviour held exactly constant across mass. |
| **B_absolute** | -1.0 | 1.0 | Absolute-scale fragmentation: fragments at fixed m/z, independent of precursor. Fractional behaviour NOT constant. |
| **C_blend** | -1.0 | 1.0 | 50/50 blend of the two, the realistic middle case. |
| **D_absolute_floor** | -0.4791 | 0.5309 | Proportional fragments with a fixed 50 Da low-mass cutoff -- proportional chemistry plus one absolute instrument limit. |

## Findings

**1. Purely fractional fragmentation manufactures nothing.** Regime A
gives rho = -0.0578 at NCE 90 — numerically zero.
When fractional behaviour is genuinely held constant, mu is mass-invariant,
confirming the analytic statement. The normalization is not intrinsically
pathological.

**2. Absolute-scale fragmentation manufactures everything.** Regime B
gives rho = -1.0 — a perfect rank association produced
entirely by the denominator, with no chemistry whatsoever.

**3. The realistic case is the concerning one.** Regime D applies purely
proportional chemistry plus a **single absolute instrument limit** — a
fixed 50 Da low-mass cutoff, which real instruments have. That alone
produces rho = -0.4791 at NCE 90, a correlation of the same
sign and a magnitude comparable to the observed -0.6759.

> **What this does and does not establish.** This is a *sensitivity and
> mechanistic-coupling* diagnostic. It shows that a mass association of
> roughly this magnitude **can** be generated with no chemistry at all.
> It does **not** identify what fraction of the observed association is
> artifactual. The diagnostic is not fitted to the data, its fragment
> programme is stipulated rather than estimated, and no counterfactual
> version of this corpus without the coupling exists. Attributing a
> percentage to the artifact would require identification this design
> does not have. The correct reading is: **mass dependence of mu is not,
> by itself, evidence of chemistry.**

**4. On the real data the association migrates into the fragment term.**
rho against precursor mass, by component:

| NCE | rho(mu, mass) | rho(phi, mass) | rho(SY, mass) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15 | -0.0913 | -0.1161 | -0.1034 |
| 30 | -0.5094 | -0.3416 | -0.5111 |
| 45 | -0.6088 | -0.5189 | -0.6236 |
| 60 | -0.6401 | -0.6075 | -0.6404 |
| 75 | -0.6588 | -0.6497 | -0.5924 |
| 90 | -0.6759 | -0.6735 | -0.5021 |

At NCE 90, rho(phi, mass) = -0.6735 is essentially identical to
rho(mu, mass) = -0.6759. The high-energy mass association is
carried by **phi — the fragment term, where the denominator lives** — not
by survival yield. That is the signature the mechanical-coupling
hypothesis predicts.

## Consequence for K5

A mass association of the observed sign and comparable magnitude **can**
arise from mu's normalization combined with an absolute low-mass limit,
with no molecular chemistry involved. Two limits on that statement matter:

- The diagnostic does **not** decompose the observed association into
  artifactual and chemical parts. It is a sensitivity result, not an
  identification result.
- The observed association *grows* with collision energy (−0.09 to −0.68)
  while regime D's is roughly flat across the grid (−0.46 to −0.53). The
  coupling mechanism alone therefore does not reproduce the observed
  energy dependence, which is evidence that the observed pattern is not
  purely mechanical.

What it does establish, and what K5 uses:

1. **Mass-dependence of mu is not, by itself, evidence of chemistry**, and
2. any structure claim resting on mass-correlated descriptors must carry
   that caveat explicitly.

Mechanically induced mass dependence is **not** treated as
molecular-structure discovery. This finding is carried into the K5
adjudication in `PHASE2_DECISION.md`.
