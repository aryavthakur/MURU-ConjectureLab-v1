# Simulated Reviewer B — Statistics and Machine Learning

**Status:** Adversarial simulated review of the Phase 4 canonical manuscript source. This is not a human peer review or an endorsement. I checked the source against the frozen MSnLib analysis script, metrics implementation, comparator analysis, project protocols, result JSON, and Phase 2 provenance records.

## Overall assessment

The primary external estimand is now described substantially more accurately than in Phase 3. The manuscript reports the right population counts, distinguishes pooled P1 from per-compound summaries, identifies the scaffold as the bootstrap unit, and makes the central practical-threshold distinction explicit: the confidence interval for the ratio is below 1 but entirely above 0.95. The least-similar quartile and tail results are not hidden. The correction that candidate/reference differences do not isolate a Morgan-fingerprint effect is statistically necessary and now appears in Methods.

The main external numerical result is supported by the frozen analysis output: candidate P1 0.1296115107, reference P1 0.1335276112, ratio 0.9706719801, scaffold-bootstrap interval [0.9523611880, 0.9892672062], 1,789 compounds and 1,687 scaffold groups. The script uses 10,000 whole-scaffold bootstrap replicates with seed 20261011, retaining the paired two-rung observations and recomputing P1 and the ratio in each replicate. These are valid conditional sampling statements for the frozen model, analysis population, endpoint, and energy interface; they do not include model-selection or training uncertainty.

I identified two remaining major editorial/statistical consistency issues and several moderate clarifications. None invalidates the stored external P1 calculation. They should be corrected before circulation so readers do not infer more independence or stronger tail criteria than the frozen protocol supports.

## Moderate concerns

1. **Abstract §Results.** The phrase “five were outside either model’s profile support” is understandable but could be read as five outside the support of both models. The frozen rule excludes a compound when either candidate or reference fails support at either rung. Prefer “five failed the joint support rule because at least one model was unsupported at one or more rungs.” This exactly captures the union rule.

2. **Methods §2.2 and Figure 1.** The 1,794→5→1,789 population flow is correct, but the supplement/table should explicitly say the five are excluded for the *joint* support rule and that the five are not incomplete measured spectra. The current manuscript says all compounds had both measured rungs in the analysis frame, which helps; keep that distinction adjacent to Figure 1.

3. **Methods §2.7.** The bootstrap description is mostly reproducible. It should say the percentile interval is conditional on the fixed trained models and does not capture model-selection/training uncertainty. This is in the Discussion and verification report but should be close to the confidence interval definition in Methods.

4. **Methods §2.7, AF_max definition.** “AF_max (proportion with any rung absolute error > 0.30)” is consistent with the code’s `nanmax(abs(E), axis=1) > 0.30`. The main paper should keep the interpretation as a frozen engineering threshold and avoid calling either AF measure a physical tail-risk probability.

5. **Results §3.6.** The prose correctly reports AF candidate 0.118502 versus reference 0.117943 and an AF-difference interval of approximately [−0.0145, +0.0155]. It correctly avoids claiming improvement or equality. The figure should not use a visual scale or annotation that implies the point difference is negligible with certainty; the new figure appropriately says no tail-risk improvement was demonstrated.

6. **Results §3.5 and Figure 4.** Q1 (n=449) ratio 1.0083 is the adverse least-similar subgroup result. It is appropriately described as a point estimate without a quartile-specific interval. Keep “least-similar” alongside “most novel” so this operational descriptor is not interpreted as novelty in all chemical senses.

7. **Results §3.7, comparator comparison.** The correction that comparator spectra were reduced to μ is important and accurate. The mapped ratios are a separate benchmark with its own 1,327-compound common population and different comparator-selection/data provenance. These results are not the primary external endpoint and their apparent differences should remain explicitly conditional on the energy inputs and modeling differences. The manuscript now says this; avoid a broad statistical-superiority conclusion from the comparator point ratios unless the corresponding comparator-bootstrap intervals and multiplicity rule are also reported.

8. **Introduction, “independently sampled external chemical-library population tests transport.”** The external draw supports a test of transfer to this replacement population, but because one sample was exposed and replaced under the project’s evolving history, “external transfer evaluation” is more exact than “independent validation.” The main Results and abstract now largely honor this distinction.

## Statistical and machine-learning checks

- **P1:** The manuscript formula is the pooled cell-weighted RMSE. Each of 1,789 compounds contributes two measured energy cells, giving 3,578 cells. This matches `muru.wur_v2.metrics.p1` and the saved analysis result.
- **Paired ratio:** Candidate and reference are scored on identical compounds/rungs under the same support rule. The reported ratio is candidate P1 divided by reference P1; <1 favors candidate. This is the correct direction.
- **Bootstrap:** `cluster_bootstrap` samples 1,687 scaffold groups with replacement using a multinomial weight vector, preserves all compounds and rung cells in each selected group, and recomputes pooled P1 and ratio. It uses 10,000 replicates, seed 20261011, percentile quantiles. The method does not represent model-training uncertainty and is conditional on the observed clusters and frozen model. This should be stated adjacent to the interval.
- **Practical threshold:** The external estimate 0.97067198 is below 1 but above 0.95; both confidence limits are above 0.95. Thus the evidence supports a directional candidate advantage under the frozen comparison, but the predeclared 5% reduction criterion fails. The paper now states this correctly and does not convert the ratio to an “accuracy” percentage.
- **Tail results:** AF contrast is +0.000559 candidate minus reference, with a 95% interval crossing zero and below the frozen +0.03 adverse threshold. Q90 and CVaR95 slightly favor the reference in point values; Q95 slightly favors candidate. No demonstrated tail-risk improvement is justified.
- **Selection bias:** Candidate selection was a multi-family development search (13 experiment families). The external replacement sample was separately drawn under an outcome-blind protocol after an exposure incident, then analyzed once; this provides useful external evaluation but does not erase the development selection process or sample-exposure history. The unreproduced max-T interval must remain out of verified inferential claims.
- **Comparator fairness:** MURU/reference differences include representation, descriptor scaling, dimensionality, hyperparameter grid, and selected penalty. The manuscript’s statement that the contrast does not isolate Morgan features is essential. Comparator systems also differ in training data, architecture and energy convention. No causal feature attribution or general model superiority follows.
- **Multiplicity and subgroup findings:** Primary P1 ratio is the designated primary outcome; per-rung, novelty, tails, and comparator analyses are secondary. Quartiles have no intervals and should remain descriptive. Do not interpret the monotonic-looking Q2–Q4 ratios or one adverse Q1 ratio as established subgroup interaction effects.

## Minor/editorial concerns

- In §2.5 the sentence “the candidate has α=.3 and block weight .1” is consistent with the frozen candidate but the manuscript should avoid implying that the same settings were selected under a one-time prespecified test; it correctly describes a multi-family development search earlier.
- Use one term consistently for the Tier A reference. “Refitted reference” should be reserved for grouped development-fold estimates; “frozen reference” for the external MSnLib use.
- “Independent external evidence” in Figure 1 is not needed to understand the flow and should be removed.
- The word “trajectory” is accurate for the two-rung response profile but should not imply dense interpolation was experimentally observed. The model interpolates through a shared fitted profile; MSnLib observations are at two rungs.

## Unresolved concerns requiring evidence beyond the current records

- The original max-T calculation cannot be reproduced without the exact code, random seed, resamples, and selection ledger. No new interval should be constructed and relabeled as the historical result.
- The external interval does not quantify uncertainty from training/model selection, and raw training source reprocessing was not part of the reviewer package.
- The physical relationship between WUR and MSnLib collision-energy settings is not established by the empirical affine map. This affects the physical interpretation and comparator ranking, though not the reproducibility of the frozen numerical analysis.
- No new independently sampled experimental cohort exists to replicate the external result.

## Recommendation

**Major revision before human review**, limited to correction of the stale max-T provenance sentence, narrower external-independence wording, unambiguous “frozen” reference wording, and full disclosure of the AF decision override. The numerical external result itself is accurately and conservatively reported, subject to the stated frozen-pipeline and energy-interface boundaries.
